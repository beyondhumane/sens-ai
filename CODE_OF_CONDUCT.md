# Code of conduct

Sens is built in the open. Anyone who takes part in it — in issues, pull requests, reviews, or anywhere the project is discussed in its name — keeps it a place where anyone else can take part too.

## Expected

- Be considerate and patient. People arrive with different languages, experience and time to give.
- Criticise code, designs and decisions, not the people who made them. Say what is wrong and, where you can, what would be better.
- Assume good faith, and accept correction when you are the one who got it wrong.
- Keep a thread on the subject of its issue or pull request.

## Not accepted

- Harassment, insults, slurs, or remarks about anyone's identity or background: gender, gender identity and expression, sexual orientation, disability, ethnicity, nationality, religion, age, appearance or level of experience.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and attention that is not welcome.
- Threats or intimidation, or encouraging others to do either.
- Publishing someone's private information — an address, an email, a name they have not shared — without their consent.
- Sustained disruption: trolling, flooding a thread, or reopening a settled decision only to derail it.

## Reporting

Open the three-dot menu at the top right of the comment, issue or pull request, choose **Report content**, then **Report to repository admins**. The report reaches the maintainers, not the thread.

For abuse that breaks GitHub's own rules — spam, threats, illegal content — report it to GitHub as well, from the same menu.

## What maintainers do

Maintainers read every report, decide from what was written, and act in proportion to it:

1. A private note, or a correction in the thread.
2. A warning, with the content edited, hidden or deleted.
3. A temporary or permanent block from the repository.

A single serious act can go straight to a block.

This code is adapted from the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/), version 2.1.
